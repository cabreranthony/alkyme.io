## WHY THIS CHANGE

**What was wrong:**
"Market-leading companies" is an overclaim we can't back up. Sevā AI has 3 paying clients and proven cost savings—that's real traction, not market leadership. Epoch² is in alpha testing. Neither is "market-leading" by any honest definition.

The Patagonia test: Would I say "market-leading companies" to Yvon Chouinard's face while looking at a 3-client healthcare platform and an alpha game engine? No. He'd call bullshit, and he'd be right.

**What's better now:**
"Real businesses with paying customers" is 100% defensible. It's honest about scale while staying confident about results. It keeps the warmth of the longer sentence structure Anthony asked for—we're not being curt, we're being inviting AND truthful.

The phrase "real businesses" does two things:
1. Acknowledges we build actual companies, not prototypes or consulting projects
2. Sets honest expectations—these are functioning businesses, not billion-dollar exits (yet)

"Paying customers" is concrete proof. It's specific without being defensive. It shows traction without overclaiming impact.

**What I learned:**
Anthony's right—brevity isn't always the answer. Warmth matters. But honesty still comes first.

I initially wanted to cut this to: "Build companies. Fast." That would have been too curt. Marcus kept the warmth with his longer explanation about shared teams and systematic processes—that educational tone helps people understand what a venture studio actually does.

My job was to fix the one dishonest claim while keeping everything else Marcus got right.

The sentence is still long. It's still warm. It's still inviting. But now it's also true.
