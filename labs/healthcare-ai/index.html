Case Study · Healthcare

From scattered to strategic: how a regional health system turned marketing into an always-on AI operation

A multi-facility regional healthcare system had been running marketing the way most community health systems do—a small outside agency, a part-time executive sponsor, and a stack of spreadsheets. We replaced that model with AI agents that plan campaigns, produce creative, buy media, and report results.

This case study covers the client's patient-facing marketing program. Physician recruitment, employer-of-choice campaigns, and donor communications were handled by separate teams and are not part of the work described here.

The Client

A regional system doing serious work with a small back office

The client operates multiple acute-care and ambulatory facilities across a defined geographic region, with a full slate of service lines ranging from primary care to specialty surgical programs. Like many health systems at this scale, it had never stood up a dedicated internal marketing function.

Multi-site

A regional footprint spanning acute-care, ambulatory, and specialty locations.

One part-time sponsor

Marketing was an add-on to a senior executive's operational role.

One small agency

A traditional creative-and-media shop, limited technical capability.

Zero internal marketers

No CMO, no brand manager, no in-house designer or media buyer.

The Challenge

Marketing was a side-of-desk function pretending to be a growth engine

Before we engaged, the system's marketing operation was held together by the goodwill of a senior executive who already had a full operational job, and an outside agency that was competent at traditional advertising but not equipped for the speed, targeting, and measurement modern healthcare marketing requires.

Where the cracks were showing

Invisible ROI

Reporting was a monthly PDF of impressions and clicks with no line back to patient volume or cost per acquired patient.

Stale media mix

Budget allocation mirrored the prior year line for line. Underperforming channels kept getting funded.

Slow creative cycles

A single flyer or digital ad could take two to three weeks from brief to approval to publish.

No brand system

Each campaign invented its own look. Typography, color, and voice drifted between service lines.

Service-line conflicts

Cardiology, orthopedics, women's health, and others competed for the same channels with no central calendar.

Compliance bottlenecks

Every asset routed through legal and clinical review manually. Turnaround measured in days, not hours.

Our Approach

We didn't build a marketing team. We built a marketing system.

Hiring a full internal marketing department would have been disproportionate to the system's scale, and replacing the agency with a bigger agency would have reproduced the same problems at a higher cost. We took a different path: design a set of narrowly-scoped AI agents, each one owning a single discipline of modern marketing, and connect them through a central orchestration layer.

The goal was never to remove humans from decisions—it was to remove humans from repetition. Strategy, approvals, and clinical judgment stay with the people who own them. Drafting, routing, testing, optimizing, reporting, and the hundred small campaign chores in between are handled by agents working on a shared brief and a shared brand system.

Six agents, one orchestrator

Each agent owns a single discipline. The orchestrator coordinates them around a shared brief.

01

Research Agent

Monitors competitors, local market trends, and service-line performance. Generates quarterly competitive positioning reports.

02

Compliance Agent

Validates all creative against HIPAA requirements, medical claim regulations, and internal clinical review standards before routing to legal.

03

Content Agent

Generates ad copy, landing pages, email campaigns, and social posts. Works from approved messaging frameworks and brand guidelines.

04

Media Agent

Plans media mix, manages budgets across channels, and executes programmatic buys. Optimizes spend based on real-time performance data.

05

Reputation Agent

Manages local listings, monitors reviews, and drafts responses. Ensures accurate facility info across 50+ platforms.

06

Reporting Agent

Aggregates performance data, calculates attribution, and produces executive dashboards. Links marketing spend to patient volume by service line.

Campaign Orchestrator

The central layer coordinating all agents. Routes briefs, manages approval workflows, and maintains the shared brand system. The executive sponsor interacts with this layer only—not individual agents.
