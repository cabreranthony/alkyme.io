How we work

Small teams, high autonomy, no bullshit. Here's what working at Alkymē actually looks like.

PHILOSOPHY

Own outcomes, not tasks

We don't hand you a spec. You shape the problem, design the solution, and own the results. If it works, you built it. If it doesn't, you learned something. No one knows the product better than you.

PHILOSOPHY

Small, senior teams

We hire people who have shipped before. Everyone can actually do the work. No juniors learning on the job. No managers managing managers. If you can't ship independently, this isn't the place.

PHILOSOPHY

Shared context, not handoffs

Product, design, engineering, and growth work in the same room. No sprint planning theater. No cross-functional alignment meetings. Just the work.

PHILOSOPHY

Fast feedback loops

We ship to real users early. You'll see your work in production within days, not quarters. Data tells us what's working. We kill what doesn't. No six-month roadmap presentations.

PHILOSOPHY

Evidence over ego

Good ideas come from anywhere. Bad ideas get killed fast, no matter who suggested them. We care about what works, not who was right. Rank doesn't win arguments—evidence does.

PHILOSOPHY

No performance theater

No daily standups to prove you're working. No deck reviews with execs who don't understand the product. No roadmap presentations to justify your existence. Build things, ship things, measure things. That's it.

What we're looking for

We don't care about your resume. We care about what you've built and how you think about problems.

01

You've shipped

You've built something people actually use. Could be a side project, a startup, or work at a company. If you've only built prototypes that never launched, this isn't the place.

02

You're technical enough

Designers should understand system constraints. PMs should read code. Marketers should set up tracking. You don't need to be an expert in everything, but you can't be helpless outside your lane.

03

You prefer building to talking

We don't do a lot of meetings. If you need alignment sessions, stakeholder updates, and status check-ins to feel productive, you'll hate it here.

04

You're comfortable with ambiguity

Early-stage ventures don't have clear requirements. You'll need to figure out what to build, how to build it, and whether it's working—often at the same time.
