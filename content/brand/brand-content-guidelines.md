## Brand Positioning

### Who We Are

Alkyme is a startup studio. We originate venture ideas, test them with real users, and build the ones that show traction into standalone companies. We operate in-house, own what we build, and use modern technology (including AI) to move faster.

We are not:
- An agency (we don't take clients)
- A consultancy (we don't sell advice)
- An accelerator (we originate, we don't invest in external founders)
- A tech vendor (we build companies, not tools for hire)

### What We Do

We identify problems worth solving, prototype solutions quickly, validate with user behavior, and scale the ventures that work. Most ideas don't make it past early testing. The ones that do become independent businesses.

### Why It Matters

Speed, clarity, and ownership. We compress the timeline from idea to launch. We make decisions based on signal, not consensus. We build things designed to last.

### Audience Segments

**Primary:**
- Builders (engineers, designers, product people looking for high-ownership roles)
- Operators (growth, ops, strategy roles who want to see impact quickly)
- Founders/entrepreneurs (curious about our model or considering joining)

**Secondary:**
- Investors (potential partners or backers of spun-out ventures)
- Press/media (covering studio model, venture launches, or tech approach)

**Tertiary:**
- Students/early-career (learning about startup studios, following our work)

## Voice & Tone

### Voice (Consistent Across All Content)

Alkyme's voice is **confident, direct, and grounded**.

- **Confident**: We know what we're doing. We've shipped products, tested ideas, and built systems that work. No hedging, no "we believe" qualifiers unless genuinely uncertain.
- **Direct**: Short sentences. Active voice. No jargon unless necessary. Say what we mean.
- **Grounded**: We talk about real outcomes, not aspirations. Metrics over mission statements. Results over rhetoric.

**Examples:**

❌ "We believe in empowering teams to innovate rapidly through cutting-edge methodologies."
✅ "We build fast, test with real users, and ship what works."

❌ "Our vision is to reimagine the future of venture creation."
✅ "We originate ideas, validate them in market, and turn traction into companies."

### Tone (Adjusts by Context)

**Website (Home, About, Ventures):** Professional, outcome-focused, narrative-driven
**Careers Page:** Honest, high-expectation, transparent about trade-offs
**Case Studies:** Story-driven, technical where relevant, focused on journey and results
**AI/Technology Pages:** Matter-of-fact, no hype, practical applications
**Social Media:** Conversational but professional, occasional dry humor, never memes
**Job Descriptions:** Clear expectations, no fluff, real day-to-day details

## Writing Principles

### 1. Show, Don't Tell

Instead of claiming attributes, demonstrate them through outcomes or specifics.

❌ "We move fast and iterate quickly."
✅ "We prototype in days, test with users in weeks, and ship or kill based on what we learn."

❌ "Our team is world-class."
✅ "Our team has shipped products at Google, Stripe, and early-stage startups."

### 2. Be Specific

Vague language weakens trust. Numbers, examples, and concrete details build credibility.

❌ "We use AI to improve our workflow."
✅ "We use AI to analyze 400 competitor sites in 90 minutes instead of two weeks."

❌ "We care about craft."
✅ "We document every system we build. Code is reviewed. Designs are tested with real users before launch."

### 3. Start with the User's Question

Every section should answer an implicit question the reader has.

- Homepage hero: *What do you do?*
- Model section: *How does this work?*
- Careers: *What's it like to work here?*
- Case study: *What did you build and why does it matter?*

### 4. Respect the Reader's Time

- No unnecessary preamble
- One idea per paragraph
- Headlines should stand alone (skim-friendly)
- Front-load the important information

### 5. Write Like You Talk (But Edited)

Conversational, but not casual. Professional, but not corporate. Read it out loud. If it sounds stiff or robotic, rewrite it.

❌ "We leverage synergistic methodologies to optimize ideation velocity."
✅ "We test ideas quickly and invest in the ones that show traction."

### 6. Vary Sentence Length

Short sentences create urgency. Medium sentences provide context. Long sentences slow the reader down to absorb complexity, but use them sparingly or you risk losing attention.

**Example:**
"Most ideas fail. That's expected. We run cheap tests, watch user behavior, and double down on what works. If an idea doesn't gain traction in the first few weeks, we move on."

## Content Structure

### Page Architecture

Every page should follow a clear narrative arc:

1. **Hook** (What is this? Why does it matter?)
2. **Context** (Background, problem, or opportunity)
3. **Depth** (How it works, what we learned, or what you'll experience)
4. **Proof** (Metrics, examples, testimonials, case studies)
5. **Action** (What should the reader do next?)

### Section Anatomy

**Eyebrow** (Optional)
Uppercase label, 8-12 characters, provides context
*Example: "Our Model" / "How We Work" / "Recent Launch"*

**Headline**
Active voice, clear benefit or outcome
*Example: "Multiple shots on goal" / "Work on things that ship"*

**Body**
2-4 sentences max per paragraph
Lead with the main point
Support with specifics or examples

**Cards/Grid Items**
Title: 3-5 words, outcome-focused
Text: 1-2 sentences, specific and concrete

**CTA**
Action-oriented, tells the user exactly what happens next
*Good: "View open roles" / "Read the case study" / "See our ventures"*
*Bad: "Learn more" / "Click here" / "Explore"*

## Word Choice & Language

### Preferred Language

Use these terms when describing what we do:

- **Studio** (not incubator, lab, or innovation center)
- **Venture** (not project, startup, or initiative)
- **Originate** (when describing idea generation)
- **Validate** (when describing market testing)
- **Traction** (early signal that something works)
- **Spin out** (when a venture becomes independent)
- **Signal** (user behavior, metrics, early indicators)
- **In-house** (everything built internally)

### Industry-Specific Terms (Use Carefully)

Acceptable in technical or case study contexts:
- Product-market fit
- MVP (minimum viable product)
- Unit economics
- CAC (customer acquisition cost)
- Retention, churn, conversion

Always define or contextualize on first use if the audience is broad.

### Banned Phrases

Never use these corporate/startup clichés:

❌ "Thought leader"
❌ "Disrupting [industry]"
❌ "Best-in-class"
❌ "Synergy" or "leverage" (as verbs)
❌ "Bleeding edge"
❌ "Rockstar" or "ninja" (in job titles)
❌ "Drink the Kool-Aid"
❌ "Move the needle"
❌ "Boil the ocean"
❌ "Circle back"
❌ "Low-hanging fruit"
❌ "Paradigm shift"

### Active vs. Passive Voice

Default to active voice. Passive voice is acceptable when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

❌ "The product was built by our team."
✅ "We built the product."

❌ "Decisions are made based on data."
✅ "We make decisions based on data."

**Exception:** Passive voice works when focusing on the outcome.
✅ "The venture was launched in March 2025."

## What We Avoid

### 1. Hype and Superlatives

We don't claim to be the "best," "fastest," or "most innovative." We describe what we've done and let the reader decide.

❌ "The world's leading startup studio"
✅ "A startup studio based in Los Angeles"

❌ "Revolutionary approach to venture building"
✅ "We build ventures in-house and spin them out when they're ready to scale"

### 2. Vague Mission Statements

No aspirational language without concrete backing.

❌ "We're reimagining the future of work."
✅ "We build tools that help teams move faster."

### 3. Over-Explaining AI

AI is part of our stack, not the story. Mention it where relevant, but don't make it the hero.

❌ "We use cutting-edge AI and machine learning to revolutionize ideation."
✅ "We use AI to compress research and prototyping timelines."

### 4. Defensiveness

We don't need to justify our approach or pre-emptively answer criticism. Confidence doesn't require disclaimers.

❌ "Unlike traditional agencies, we don't just consult—we actually build."
✅ "We build our own ventures. No clients, no retainers."

### 5. Filler Words and Hedging

Remove words that add no meaning: "really," "very," "quite," "just," "simply," "actually," "basically."

❌ "We're really focused on building things that actually work."
✅ "We build things that work."

### 6. Buzzword Bingo

If a sentence reads like it could be on any startup's website, rewrite it.

❌ "We leverage data-driven insights to drive innovation at scale."
✅ "We test ideas with real users and scale what works."

## Content Types & Templates

### 1. Case Study

**Structure:**
- **The Opportunity**: What problem or space did we explore?
- **What We Built**: Description of the product/venture (high-level, not exhaustive)
- **Why It Matters**: Business value, user impact, or technical achievement
- **The Build**: Key systems, decisions, or technical approach (if relevant)
- **What We Learned**: Honest takeaways (challenges, surprises, what worked)
- **The Results**: Metrics, outcomes, current status
- **What's Next**: Future plans or how it evolved

**Tone:** Narrative, technical where relevant, honest about trade-offs

**Example Opening:**
"Real-time strategy games are expensive to build and locked behind download barriers. We saw an opportunity: modern browsers can handle complex game logic, and WebRTC enables multiplayer without servers. Could we build a production-ready RTS engine that runs entirely in the browser?"

### 2. Job Description

**Structure:**
- **Role Title** (clear, not creative)
- **What You'll Do** (real day-to-day, not aspirational tasks)
- **What We're Looking For** (skills, experience, mindset)
- **What You Should Know** (honest about pace, expectations, trade-offs)
- **How to Apply** (clear next steps)

**Tone:** Direct, high-expectation, transparent

**Example Snippet:**
"You'll own growth for an early-stage B2B product. That means running acquisition experiments, analyzing user behavior, and deciding what channels are worth scaling. You'll work directly with product and engineering. Priorities will shift. You'll be expected to figure some of it out as you go."

### 3. Launch Announcement

**Structure:**
- **What We Launched** (product/venture name and one-sentence description)
- **The Problem** (why this matters)
- **What It Does** (key features or value props)
- **Who It's For** (target user)
- **What's Next** (roadmap or call to action)

**Tone:** Confident but understated, outcome-focused

**Example:**
"Today we're launching [Venture Name], a tool that helps [user] do [outcome]. Most [existing solutions] are [problem]. We built [solution] to [benefit]. It's live now at [URL]."

### 4. About/Company Page

**Structure:**
- **Who We Are** (1-2 sentences, high-level identity)
- **What We Do** (process, model, approach)
- **How We Work** (principles, values in action)
- **Where We Are** (location, size, stage)

**Tone:** Professional, grounded, minimal self-promotion

### 5. Testimonials/Quotes

**Structure:**
"[Quote text in plain language, no corporate speak]"
— [First Name Last Initial], [Role], [Location]

**Guidelines:**
- Keep quotes short (1-2 sentences max)
- Focus on specific outcomes or experiences, not platitudes
- Avoid "amazing," "incredible," or other hype words
- Ensure diversity in roles and locations

**Good Example:**
"You're not working on slides. You're working on real products that ship."
— Max R., Growth Lead, Seattle

**Bad Example:**
"Working at Alkyme has been an incredible journey and I've learned so much from this amazing team."

## Editorial Standards

### Grammar & Mechanics

- **Oxford comma:** Yes, always.
- **Contractions:** Allowed (we're, don't, it's) for conversational tone.
- **Numbers:** Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10+. Exception: Always use numerals for metrics (5 users, 3 features).
- **Percentages:** Use numerals with % symbol (15%, not fifteen percent).
- **Dates:** Month Day, Year (March 15, 2025) or Month Year (March 2025).
- **Time zones:** PT, ET (not PST/PDT—simpler for global audience).
- **Currency:** USD by default. Use $ symbol ($100, not $100 USD) unless comparing currencies.

### Capitalization

- **Venture names:** Title case (ChronoCore, not chronocore).
- **Job titles in headlines:** Title case (Product Manager). In sentences: lowercase (hired a product manager).
- **Alkyme:** Always capitalized, with proper accent (Alkymē) in formal contexts; plain "Alkyme" acceptable in URLs/code.
- **AI:** All caps (AI, not ai or A.I.).
- **Headings:** Sentence case preferred ("How we work" not "How We Work").

### Punctuation

- **Em dashes:** Use sparingly. Prefer parentheses, commas, or split into two sentences.
- **Semicolons:** Acceptable for connecting closely related independent clauses. Don't overuse.
- **Ellipses:** Avoid unless quoting someone who trailed off. Never for dramatic effect.
- **Exclamation points:** Almost never. Reserve for genuine surprise or error messages.

### Formatting

- **Bold:** Use for emphasis on key terms, section labels, or important callouts. Don't overuse (max 1-2 per paragraph).
- **Italics:** Use for emphasis, book/publication titles, or introducing new terms.
- **ALL CAPS:** Only for acronyms (CEO, API, MVP) or eyebrow labels.
- **Links:** Descriptive anchor text ("Read the case study" not "Click here"). Open external links in new tab.

### Acronyms

- Spell out on first use with acronym in parentheses: "minimum viable product (MVP)"
- Use acronym thereafter: "We shipped the MVP in two weeks."
- Exception: AI, CEO, CTO (assume known).

## SEO & Metadata

### Page Titles

Format: `[Primary Keyword] | Alkyme`

Examples:
- "Startup Studio | Alkyme"
- "Careers at Alkyme | Join Our Team"
- "ChronoCore Case Study | Alkyme"

**Character limit:** 50-60 characters (including " | Alkyme")

### Meta Descriptions

- 140-155 characters
- Active voice, clear value proposition
- Include primary keyword naturally
- End with a call to action or benefit

**Example:**
"Alkyme builds companies from scratch. We originate ideas, test with real users, and launch ventures designed to scale."

### Headers (H1-H6)

- **H1:** One per page, primary keyword, clear user value
- **H2:** Section headers, scannable, keyword-rich where natural
- **H3-H6:** Subsections, supportive hierarchy

**Do not keyword-stuff.** Write for humans first, search engines second.

### Image Alt Text

Descriptive, concise, keyword-relevant when natural.

❌ "image-001.jpg"
❌ "startup studio venture building innovation"
✅ "Alkyme team collaborating on new venture prototype"

### URL Structure

- Lowercase, hyphen-separated
- Descriptive, keyword-relevant
- Short and clean

**Good:**
- `/ventures`
- `/careers`
- `/case-studies/chronocore`

**Bad:**
- `/page1`
- `/careers_page_final_v2`
- `/blog/2025/03/15/startup-studio-venture-building-innovation-los-angeles`

## Approval Process

### Who Reviews What

**Website Copy (Home, About, Ventures, Careers):**
- Draft: Content lead or PM
- Review: Founder + 1 senior team member
- Approval: Founder

**Case Studies:**
- Draft: PM or lead who worked on venture
- Review: Technical lead (for accuracy) + Content lead (for brand alignment)
- Approval: Founder

**Job Descriptions:**
- Draft: Hiring manager
- Review: Content lead (for tone/clarity)
- Approval: Hiring manager + Founder (for senior roles)

**Social Media / Blog Posts:**
- Draft: Responsible team member
- Review: Content lead
- Approval: Auto-publish after review (unless sensitive)

**Press Releases / Announcements:**
- Draft: Content lead or comms lead
- Review: Founder + leadership team
- Approval: Founder

### Review Checklist

Before publishing any content:

- [ ] Aligns with brand voice (confident, direct, grounded)
- [ ] Answers the reader's implicit question
- [ ] No banned phrases or corporate clichés
- [ ] Specific examples or metrics where relevant
- [ ] Active voice unless passive is intentional
- [ ] Scannable (headlines, bullets, short paragraphs)
- [ ] Clear call to action
- [ ] Grammar and spelling checked
- [ ] Links tested (no 404s)
- [ ] Meta title and description optimized
- [ ] Alt text on all images
- [ ] Mobile-friendly formatting
