Approach

We build with constraints, not assumptions

Most ideas sound good in theory. We focus on what holds up under real conditions.

Every idea starts with constraints. Who actually needs this, how often, and why now.

We look for problems that are clear, repeatable, and tied to real behavior. If it only works in a pitch, it does not move forward.

As we test ideas, we prioritize signal over certainty. Early traction matters more than polished strategy.

When something works, we invest quickly. When it does not, we stop just as quickly.

What this means in practice

  • We do not build without a clear user and use case
  • We test before committing resources
  • We prioritize speed over completeness
  • We make decisions based on behavior, not opinions

Our model

How we decide what to build

Most bets never leave thesis. The ones that do earn their next stage with proof, not paperwork.

We originate our own ideas—problems we want to own, not pitches from the outside.

We test demand before scaling effort. Behavior in market beats internal consensus.

We invest in what shows traction—more time, capital, and team when the numbers support it.

We shut down what does not. No sunk-cost gravity—evidence decides.
