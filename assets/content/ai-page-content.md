**Revised (narrative/copywriter version):**

What this actually looks like

**Research: 500 competitor pages in 90 minutes**
We point AI at every competitor landing page we can find. It spots patterns we'd miss—pricing tiers, feature clusters, positioning angles. We read the summary, pick three gaps worth testing, and validate with real users. What used to take a week now takes an afternoon.

**Product: 10 wireframes while you sleep**
Describe a flow. AI generates ten layout variations by morning. Most are garbage. Two are interesting. One gets user-tested with click heatmaps. The winner ships in days. The losers get deleted. No one gets precious about ideas that don't work.

**Operations: Meetings you'll never transcribe again**
AI writes the notes. Pulls action items from Slack threads. Searches 200+ docs when someone asks "where did we put that?" It's not magic—it's just faster than Ctrl+F and more reliable than your memory.

**Content: Let AI fail so you don't have to**
Draft five subject lines. A/B test all five. AI writes fast but weird. Humans edit for tone. The best-performing line is usually 60% AI, 40% human. That's fine—what matters is the open rate, not who gets credit.

**Why narrative version works:**
- "Most are garbage" = honest, no AI hype
- "No one gets precious about ideas" = shows culture, not just process
- "More reliable than your memory" = relatable, human
- "Who gets credit" = addresses ego/ownership concerns
- Story-driven (before/after implied in each section)

**Consolidated version (matter-of-fact):**

H2
How we use AI responsibly

**Do you let AI make decisions?**
No. AI generates options. Humans evaluate, test, and decide. Example: AI drafts 10 landing page headlines → we A/B test 3 → we ship the winner based on conversion data.

**How do you validate AI outputs?**
Everything gets tested with real users or real data before shipping. We don't publish AI-generated content without human review. We don't launch features without usage signals.

**What about user data?**
We do not use customer or partner data to train AI models. Internal docs and anonymized usage patterns only.

**Where do you draw the line?**
We won't:
- Automate decisions that affect people (hiring, pricing, venture investment)
- Ship AI outputs without human review
- Use AI to replace judgment on subjective/ethical questions

**Consolidated version (narrative/copywriter tone):**

H2
The rules we set (and why)

**Does AI make decisions for you?**
Hell no. AI suggests. We test. Users decide. Example: AI writes ten landing page headlines. We A/B test three. The one with the highest conversion rate wins. Not because AI wrote it, but because real people clicked it.

**How do you know AI isn't feeding you garbage?**
Because we don't ship anything untested. AI-generated copy gets edited. AI-suggested features get validated with real users. If it can't survive contact with reality, it doesn't ship. Simple as that.

**What about our data?**
We don't train AI models on customer data. Period. Internal docs, anonymized patterns—fine. But we're not feeding your emails or user behavior into a training pipeline. That's not how we work.

**Where's the line?**
Some stuff AI should never touch:
- Hiring decisions (humans judge humans)
- Pricing strategy (affects people's livelihoods)
- Investment calls (our money, our gut)
- Anything where "because the model said so" isn't good enough

If a decision has ethical weight or affects real people, a human makes it. AI can inform that call, but it doesn't get the final say.

**Why narrative tone works:**
- "Hell no" = confident, conversational
- "Feeding you garbage" = honest about AI risks
- "Period" = draws hard line, builds trust
- "Our money, our gut" = shows skin in the game
- Addresses skepticism head-on vs sounding defensive
