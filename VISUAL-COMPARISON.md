## The Smart "Enterprise" Uses (KEEPING)

### Example 1: Positioning Against Enterprise
```
"Enterprise-grade tech, not enterprise-grade pricing"
                ▼                        ▼
          QUALITY (good)             COST (bad)
```

**Why we keep it:** Uses "enterprise" as a quality benchmark while rejecting the cost model. Perfect anti-bloat positioning.

### Example 2: Competitive Differentiation
```
"Most legal AI tools target enterprise—we're building for the long tail."
                               ▼
                    THEY serve enterprise (not us)
```

**Why we keep it:** Explicitly states we DON'T target enterprise. Positions us as the alternative.

## Risk Assessment

### What Could Go Wrong?

**Risk 1:** "Dumbing down" perception
- **Mitigation:** Keep "production-ready," "battle-tested," "SOC 2 compliant"
- **Reality:** Technical quality signals remain strong

**Risk 2:** Losing enterprise clients who DO come
- **Mitigation:** Keep "Enterprise-grade tech, not enterprise-grade pricing"
- **Reality:** Message is "we're better than enterprise," not "we don't serve enterprise"

**Risk 3:** Too casual?
- **Mitigation:** Maintain professional tone, just more accessible
- **Reality:** "Solutions" and "your business" are still professional

### Actual Risk Level: LOW
- Simple word swaps
- No structural changes
- Easy to revert if needed
- Maintains technical credibility
